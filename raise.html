    Questions

    Good questions to ask.

    Can I buy beef without raising a whole steer?

    Yes. The Ranch Store sells Texas beef by the cut, along with chicken and other products from our partner farms. Join the list to hear when it opens.

    What does it cost?

    Calves start at $3,000 for Angus or Brangus. After that you pay for feed and vet visits as you go, plus processing at the end. Add a custom diet with our nutritionist, the live camera, monthly reports, DNA verification or ranch days if you want them. Fully loaded builds can reach $10,000 or more.

    Who owns the animal?

    You do, from the day you claim it. We board, feed and care for it under a written agreement. Think of it like boarding a horse, except at the end you get a freezer full of beef.

    Where is the ranch?

    On a working cattle ranch in Texas run by our ranching partner. Owners can book ranch days to visit in person.

    How much beef will I get?

    It depends on the animal's size, its finish and your cut sheet. University extension examples range from about 480 lb of take-home beef from a 1,200 lb steer to 638 lb of retail cuts from a 1,300 lb steer.

    How long does it take?

    About 18 to 24 months from birth for a grain-finished Angus. Grass-finished steers usually take longer, and Akaushi or Wagyu need a longer finish to marble.

    When does my diet start?

    At weaning, around 6 to 8 months. Before that your calf nurses on its mother. We'll tell you what the mother is fed, too.

    What if my calf gets sick or dies?

    Our vet treats it and you're told the same day. Your agreement spells out what happens if an animal is lost, including your replacement options.

    What if I change my mind?

    It happens. Your agreement lists your options, such as returning the animal to the herd, continuing to board it, or selling it at market.

    What paperwork is involved?

    A bill of sale when you claim your calf, and a boarding agreement that covers its care, feed and vet visits. Both live in your owner portal.

    Why do my packages say Not for Sale?

    Because the beef is yours. Texas rules for custom-processed beef require every package from an animal you own to be labeled Not for Sale, and it can't be resold.

    How do I know it's really my beef?

    One tag number follows your animal from birth to every package. It's processed on its own and ground separately, and Premium owners get DNA verification.
